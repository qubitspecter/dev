Managed service provider

Every admin session, approved by the client

When a peer MSP was pursued by a client's insurer after an incident, this MSP looked at its own position. Its engineers held standing admin rights in every client tenancy, so one compromised account could reach them all, and it had no way to prove any session was authorised. About ten weeks later, every admin session was approved by the client and came from a known, MSP-owned device. Clients had nothing to install or change, and the MSP obtained cyber insurance for the first time.

1. Why the MSP acted

The MSP was focused on keeping its clients running, not on building a security practice. That changed when it learned another MSP was being pursued by a client's insurer after an incident at that client.

Its own exposure was clear. Admin access to client tenancies made it a possible route into every client, and a possible party to any claim. It engaged Decoheron to design an administration model it could stand behind with clients and insurers, without adding technical or licensing requirements for those clients.

2. What we found

  • Standing admin rights everywhere. Engineers held permanent admin rights in every client tenancy, available whether or not they were needed. One compromised account or device could reach every client.
  • No proof of authorisation. Clients weren't part of the access process, so the MSP couldn't show that any given session had been approved.
  • No device control. Admin access wasn't tied to a known device.
  • Shared exposure. An incident at a single client could draw the MSP into an insurance claim.

4. What changed

For the MSP

It obtained cyber insurance for the first time, without carrying the full exposure of every client tenancy. It can now show any client or insurer that every admin session was approved by the client and came from a known device. That addresses the exposure behind the project, without turning an operations business into a security program.

For clients

Clients had nothing to install or change. Nothing happens in their tenancy that they didn't approve.

Day to day

Engineers work from a Windows 365 PAW the MSP owns, so access depends on a known device as well as credentials. Clients keep standard office hours, so approvals fit the working day, and after-hours changes go ahead only when the client's approver is available. A small number of clients with specific needs are handled through app registrations (application identities holding only the permissions they need).
